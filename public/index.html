01Role Definition

When your decision-makers quietly picture different wins, you hire someone who succeeds at the wrong job, and everyone calls it a bad hire six months later.

What the blueprint fixes: Align every decision-maker on the business problem and a written definition of year-one success before the search advances.

  1. What best describes your current hiring situation?

    Choose the one that feels most true right now.

  2. How aligned are your key stakeholders on what this hire needs to accomplish in year one?

    1 = we have never had that conversation, 5 = fully locked in

02Outcome Scorecard

When interviewers are measuring different things, strong candidates get rejected and weak ones advance, and you won't catch it until it's on payroll.

What the blueprint fixes: Build one shared outcome scorecard and align every interviewer before the search advances.

  1. How clearly defined is your hiring scorecard for this role?

    Be honest. This is where many searches quietly fail.

  2. Is your compensation range aligned to the caliber of talent you actually need?

    The market answers this question even when internal stakeholders do not.

03Candidate Experience

Every silent week and inconsistent interview tells your best candidates you're not serious, so they take the other offer before you've decided.

What the blueprint fixes: Review every candidate touchpoint from first contact to offer. Fix the friction before the next strong candidate enters the process.

  1. How would you rate your candidate experience right now?

    Think about how the process feels to a high performer seeing it for the first time.

  2. How quickly and consistently do candidates hear back after each stage of your process?

    Silence is a decision. Top candidates interpret it as one.

04Interview & Human Fit

Hire for résumé over how someone leads under pressure, and you get a leader who looks right on paper and stalls the moment it gets hard.

What the blueprint fixes: Build a human alignment profile that defines the leadership style that succeeds under pressure and the evidence interviewers should seek.

  1. How clearly can you articulate the human traits that predict success in this role beyond skills and experience?

    1 = we have never gone there, 5 = we have a precise profile

  2. When top candidates disengage late in your process, what do you typically assume?

    Your answer reveals a lot about current blind spots.

    Hiring Clarity Blueprint.

    Every blind spot the diagnostic found has its fix in here. We've opened to the lever that's costing you most, so start there. Each module is a focused leadership conversation, not a course you must complete.

    Module 01

    Role Definition.
    What problem are you actually solving?

    Most job descriptions describe the last person who held the role, not the problem the next person needs to solve. This module forces the clarity most searches skip.

    The Business Reality Check

    Define what is breaking or at risk because this seat is empty or wrong, and what the business needs to be true in 12 months.

    The Failure Autopsy

    Understand what happened with the last leader, or which capability gap created the need for a new role.

    The Stakeholder Alignment Test

    Ask every key stakeholder separately what year-one success looks like, then resolve material differences.

    The Internal Environment Scan

    Identify the behaviors actually rewarded internally and the leadership styles that struggle in this culture.

    Do not start with the leader. Start with the problem.
    Ask yourself the following four questions...

    What happens to the business if this role stays open another six months?
    What kind of leader has failed here, and what specifically went wrong?
    What does year-one success look like in measurable terms, not adjectives?
    Where are you willing to flex on requirements, and where are you not?

    Module 02

    Outcome Scorecard.
    Rate what actually matters.

    Rate each outcome from 1, low importance, to 5, non-negotiable. Meaning comes from the distribution, not the total alone: your 5s become interview anchors, while low-rated outcomes should not distract the hiring team.

    Rating total, not a pass/fail score: 0 / 35

    Complete the scorecard to reveal your priorities.

    For the strongest insight, ask each stakeholder to complete this independently, then compare where ratings differ by two or more points.

    Module 03

    Candidate Experience Audit.
    What is your process telling them?

    The best sales leaders decide whether they trust your company long before the offer stage. Answer honestly for your current process, not the process you intend to have.

    6 to go.

    Answer for the process you run today, not the one you intend to build.

    Module 04

    Interview & Human Fit.
    Turn clarity into a consistent decision.

    The diagnostic identifies risk. This module prevents that risk from returning during interviews by assigning evidence, ownership, and decision rules before candidates enter the process.

    Assign Evidence Owners

    Give each interviewer specific outcomes and capabilities to validate instead of asking everyone to judge everything.

    Define Proof Before Interviews

    Agree on what strong, weak, and disqualifying evidence looks like for every non-negotiable criterion.

    Protect Independent Judgment

    Require written ratings before the debrief so the loudest stakeholder does not reshape everyone else's view.

    Set the Decision Rule

    Decide which gaps are coachable, which require more evidence, and which automatically stop the process.

    Evidence beats impressions.
    Ask yourselves the following five questions...

    Who owns validation of each year-one outcome?
    What evidence would cause us to change our mind about a candidate?
    Which criteria require unanimous agreement?
    How will we capture candidate feedback and process friction?
    Which human traits predict success here under pressure, and what evidence proves them?
